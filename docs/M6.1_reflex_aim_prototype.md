# Castle Siege — M6.1 Reflex Aim prototype (experimental)

**Status: experimental prototype, awaiting Project Manager review.** It lives only on the branch
`experiment/m6.1-reflex-aim`, made from `2da2db5`. It is not merged into the submission branch.
The submitted game code (`a4ceef8`) and the 82-page report are unchanged there.

## 1. What it is

Reflex Aim is an optional way for a person to aim; Classic Aim stays the default.

| | Classic Aim (default, unchanged) | Reflex Aim (new, optional) |
|---|---|---|
| Angle | Up/Down keys, 5°–80° | swings by itself, 20° → 75° → 20°, every 3 s |
| Power | Left/Right keys (Shift: ×5) | the same |
| Firing | ATTACK, SPACE (or Power Shot) fires the aim that is set | ATTACK, SPACE (or Power Shot) **locks the angle shown at that moment** and fires it |
| Who uses it | everyone | the people only: LEFT against the computer, both teams in two-player mode |
| The computer | its own aim search, unchanged | the same: it never swings |

**How to try it:** `git switch experiment/m6.1-reflex-aim`, then `python main.py`. On the
selection screen, choose **Aim: Reflex**.

## 2. How it works

- **The swing** (`reflex_aim.py`, class `AimSwing`):
  - The angle on each frame is `low + (high − low) × (1 − cos(2π · frame / period)) / 2`, with
    low 20°, high 75° and a period of 180 frames (3 s at 60 FPS). All three can be changed.
  - The barrel slows into each limit, turns and speeds up again, like a pendulum. There is no
    jump and no sudden change of speed at either end; the fastest movement is about 1° per frame
    in the middle.
  - The motion counts frames, like the shot's flight in `battle.py`, so it is the same every
    time. The shown and fired angle is the swing's angle rounded to whole degrees, the unit the
    arrow keys use.
  - The module uses only the standard library.
- **In the game** (`main.py`):
  - On each frame of a person's turn, `Game.update()` moves that team's swing one frame and
    turns the real cannon to it with the cannon's own methods (`aim_cannon`).
  - Everything that shows the aim reads the cannon, so the barrel, the arrow, the label under
    the cannon and the panel's "Angle … Power …" all follow the swing.
- **Capture:**
  - ATTACK, SPACE or Power Shot uses the existing path: the `PendingShot` records the cannon's
    angle when the input is accepted. That is the angle drawn on the frame the player saw.
  - The run to the cannon then locks the controls, so the swing stops and the barrel stays at
    the locked angle.
  - When the character reaches the cannon, `Battle.perform_action()` fires the shot once, with
    that angle.
- **Locking:** the swing moves only while the controls are open (`Game.aim_swings()` is
  `not controls_locked()`). It stands still during:
  - the run to the cannon and back;
  - the flight;
  - the computer's turn;
  - the result screen.

  Each team's swing pauses and later goes on from where it stopped, so a barrel never jumps at
  the start of a turn.
- **New match, rematch, R:** both swings start again at 20° (each person's barrel is turned
  there when the match begins). The Aim mode is kept for rematches. The other rules (first side
  alternates, R cancels a run) are unchanged.
- **Not changed:**
  - `battle.py`, `ai.py`, `characters.py`, `castle.py`, `animation.py`, `drawing.py`,
    `sound.py`;
  - gravity, damage, collision, cover, turn order;
  - the computer's aim search and difficulty settings.

## 3. On screen

- **Selection screen:**
  - an "Aim: Classic | Reflex" choice next to the team size;
  - a message explaining the chosen mode;
  - one help line at the bottom.
- **Battle, Reflex Aim only:**
  - small dots around the cannon mark the swing's range, with slightly bigger dots at 20° and
    75°;
  - the turn banner says "time your shot";
  - the opening message says ATTACK or SPACE locks the angle;
  - the panel hint says "ATTACK or SPACE locks the swinging angle. Arrows: power."

  "LEFT locked the angle at 45° (power 83)."

- **Classic Aim:** its texts and behaviour are identical to the submitted version, and a test
  checks the exact strings.

## 7. Is it too difficult against the default cover?

**Method:**
- A throwaway script fired every aim (angle 5°–80°, power 10–100) through the real `Battle`
  rules, at a RIGHT team behind the default cover.
- It then played the swing frame by frame: on how many of the 180 frames would SPACE fire a shot
  that damages a character?
- The field is a mirror image, so RIGHT shooting at LEFT gives the same numbers.

| Target | Powers that can hit | Best power: frames of 180 that hit | Each chance lasts | Median over the powers that hit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The front character (the only target in 1v1) | 18 (83–100) | power 84: 36 (20%) | 18 frames = 0.30 s, twice per swing | 14 frames |
| Any of three characters (3v3) | 18 (83–100) | power 87: 60 (33%) | 30 frames = 0.50 s, twice per swing | 28 frames |

- **No hitting aim is lost:** every aim that hits the default cover in Classic Aim lies inside
  20°–75°.
- **At the right power, the chance is not short.** Aiming at the front character at power 84,
  any press during a 0.3 s stretch hits, and that stretch comes round twice every 3 s. At the
  power the tests use (83) it is 0.22 s.
- **At other powers it is harder:**
  - the hitting angles split into two short stretches of 4–9 frames (0.07–0.15 s) per pass;
  - at some powers, a single frame.
- **Where the hard part sits:**
  - The hitting angles sit around 39°–55°, which is where the barrel moves fastest (about 0.95°
    per frame).
  - Finding the power is as hard as in Classic Aim; what is new is the timing.
- **Conclusion:** the numbers suggest Reflex Aim is playable against the default cover if the
  player picks a power near 83–87, but noticeably harder than Classic Aim, where a known aim
  always hits.
- **Not established:** whether it is *too* difficult needs a person to play it; nobody has.
- **If it proves too hard:**
  - a longer period (for example 4 s) lengthens every chance by a third;
  - a narrower range also slows the barrel, as long as it still holds the useful angles.

  Both are settings of `AimSwing`.

## 8. Fairness against the computer

1. **The computer does not have to time anything.**
   - It still searches its aims exactly and fires them with its fixed error: Easy ±4°,
     Normal ±3°, Hard ±1°. Those errors were tuned against a person using Classic Aim.
   - In Reflex Aim the person's error depends on timing: about 1° for each frame early or late
     in the middle of the swing.
   - So the difficulty levels are no longer calibrated: Reflex Aim makes every level relatively
     harder for the person.
2. **The computer can use angles the person can't.** It aims at 5°–80°; a person in Reflex Aim
   only gets 20°–75°.
   - Against the default cover no hitting aim is outside 20°–75° (§7).
   - Other cover layouts were not checked.
3. **Two players are treated alike:** each has their own swing, the same range and the same
   period, and both start at 20°.
4. **A predictable swing:** it restarts at 20° in each match and is the same every time, so a
   player could learn its rhythm. This is a design choice to review, not a fault.

The computer's behaviour was deliberately not changed (the brief: "keep the AI's existing aim
search and difficulty settings unchanged").

## 9. Open points for the Project Manager

- **No human play-test.** The difficulty, the 3 s period and whether the range dots and texts
  are clear still need a person to play.
- **Guarded Strike hint:** when a character has Guarded Strike ready, the panel's last line
  shows that hint instead of the Reflex hint. The banner still says "time your shot".
- **Frame-based motion:** if the game ran below 60 FPS, the swing would slow down with
  everything else, as the shot's flight already does.
- **The computer:** whether it should get a Reflex-style aiming error, or a rebalanced
  difficulty, is a question for a later milestone.

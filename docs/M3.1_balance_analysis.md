# M3.1 balance analysis: Defend and Engineer Repair

**Status: analysis only. No rule, number or victory condition was changed in M3.1.** Every
change suggested here needs Project Manager approval (proposed for M3.2).

The human tester reported that Defend and the Engineer's Repair feel strategically weak. This
document checks that claim in two ways:
- **with the current numbers** (§1);
- **with seeded AI-vs-AI experiments** (§2). They used the M3.1 AI at Hard, so the aiming skill is
  the same for both sides, and they ran in a throwaway script. The rule variants in §2 were
  patched in memory for the experiment only; the project code is unchanged.

## 1. The numbers under the current rules

| Option on a turn | What it achieves | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Attack on the enemy castle** (the AI's usual choice) | A tower hit damages 4 blocks: 200 HP (Engineer: 240; Power Shot: 300). The castle falls after 900 HP, so one hit is 22–33 % of a castle win | — |
| **Attack on a character** | ATK − DEF: 18–36 HP (Power Shot 45–60). A team has 90–140 HP (1v1) up to 340 HP (3v3) | The targets are small and behind the castle, so they are hit less often (Hard: 66 % in 1v1, 85 % in 3v3) |
| **Defend** | Halves the damage to **one** character from **one** enemy action: it saves 9–18 HP (30 HP against a Power Shot). It saves **nothing** if the enemy shoots the castle | The whole turn: one attack (≈ 200 castle HP) is given up |
| **Rally** (Knight) | The same for every living ally: up to about 50 HP in 3v3, if the enemy hits the group | The turn, plus a cooldown of 4 rounds |
| **Repair** (Engineer) | Up to 75 castle HP, at most once every 4 rounds: about ⅓ of one enemy tower hit | The turn: our own ≈ 200-HP hit is given up. Net effect on the castle race: about −125 HP or worse |

**Opportunity cost.** With one action per team turn, every Defend or Repair is an attack you
don't make. A castle needs only about 4–5 good hits to fall, so one lost attack is roughly a
fifth of a win. Defend and Repair give back much less than that.

**Character damage reduction.** Defend only protects characters. Under the 60 % castle rule,
both sides mostly shoot castles, so characters are rarely hurt. The AI's "below half HP"
defending therefore seldom triggers, and Defend rarely has anything to prevent.

**Castle damage versus Repair.**
- One Repair (75 HP) undoes about a third of one enemy hit (200–240 HP), and it can only be
  used every 4 rounds.
- At the end of a match, about 71 % of the lost castle HP is in *destroyed* blocks, which Repair
  can't restore. This was measured over 18 Hard-vs-Hard matches with 26 damaged castles.
- During a match, up to ≈ 350 HP is still repairable at one moment. So the limit that matters is
  the small 75-HP pool and the turn it costs, not a lack of damaged blocks.

**The 60 % castle-loss condition.** This rule makes the castle the best target:
- it is big and fixed, and a tower hit removes 13–16 % of it;
- characters are small, stand behind the castle, and have 340 HP in total in 3v3.

As a result, in the experiments below every 3v3 match and most 1v1 and 2v2 matches were won by
the castle rule. Elimination-only matches in 3v3 took about twice as long (8.8 rounds instead of
4.5).

## 2. Seeded AI-vs-AI experiments
**Setup.** 480 matches. Three policies played against each other; aiming is identical in all
three:
- **aggressive:** never defends, rallies or repairs;
- **course:** the M3.1 AI as shipped. It defends more when below half HP, uses Rally, and
  repairs when that is worth more than its attack;
- **defensive30:** defends 30 % of the time, but never twice in a row.

Each pairing was played with the sides swapped, so first-move advantage cancels out. Each cell
is 16 matches.

Rosters were fixed:
- LEFT: Knight / Archer / Engineer, as many as the team size;
- RIGHT: Archer / Engineer / Knight, as many as the team size.

So in 1v1 nobody has an Engineer, and in 2v2 only RIGHT has one.

The rule variants were patched in memory for the experiment only:

| Variant | Rules |
|---|---|
| S0 | the current rules |
| S1 | Repair restores 150 instead of 75 |
| S2 | while a team has a defending character, its castle takes half block damage |
| S3 | elimination only (castle loss off) |

**Wins of the policy against "aggressive"** (W–L over 16 matches; 50 % would mean "as good as
attacking"):

| Variant | Policy | 1v1 | 2v2 | 3v3 | All sizes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 current rules | course | 6–10 | 10–6 | 7–9 | 23–25 (48 %) |
| S0 current rules | defensive30 | 5–11 | 9–7 | 4–12 | **18–30 (38 %)** |
| S1 Repair 150 | course | 6–10 | 10–6 | 9–7 | 25–23 (52 %) |
| S1 Repair 150 | defensive30 | 5–11 | 9–7 | 4–12 | 18–30 (38 %) |
| S2 Defend also halves castle damage | course | 6–10 | 8–8 | 9–7 | 23–25 (48 %) |
| S2 Defend also halves castle damage | defensive30 | 5–11 | 10–6 | 7–9 | **22–26 (46 %)** |
| S3 elimination only | course | 6–10 | 4–12 | 4–12 | **14–34 (29 %)** |
| S3 elimination only | defensive30 | 6–10 | 5–11 | 6–10 | 17–31 (35 %) |

**Other measurements** (current rules, S0):
- **How often the course AI defends:** 9 % of its turns in 1v1, 16 % in 2v2, and 0 % in 3v3.
  In 3v3 nobody fell below half HP, because everyone shot the castle.
- **Character damage that Defend prevented:** roughly 0–130 HP in total over 16 matches.
- **Repairs:** 0–5 per 16 matches.
- **Match length:** 4.5–5 rounds.
- **First-move advantage:** inconclusive at these sample sizes. In the aggressive mirror match,
  LEFT won 2, 5 and 4 of 8.

**Caution.** 16 matches per cell gives a standard error of about ±12 percentage points per
cell. The "All sizes" column (48 matches) is the more reliable one. These are AI players;
people may value Defend and Repair differently.

## 3. Conclusions
1. **The tester is right.** Under the current rules, a Defend turn is worth less than an Attack
   turn almost every time. The more an AI defends, the more it loses (38 % for defensive30). The
   course-required "defend more below half HP" behaviour is roughly neutral only because, with
   castle victory, it seldom triggers.
2. **Repair is too small.** 75 HP is a third of one enemy hit, once every 4 rounds, and it costs
   your own hit. Doubling it to 150 (S1) helped only a little. In the 3v3 cell it went from 7–9
   to 9–7, which is within the noise.
3. **The 60 % castle rule hides the problem.** Matches are castle races, and Defend protects only
   characters.
4. **Elimination alone doesn't save Defend.** In S3, characters are the only targets and Defend
   prevents far more damage (180–690 HP per 16 matches), yet defending policies still lost more
   (29–35 %). Halving one hit on one character simply doesn't pay for a whole lost attack.
5. **Letting Defend protect the castle (S2) helped the most** (38 % → 46 %), but it still doesn't
   reach "as good as attacking".

## 4. Preliminary recommendations for M3.2 (not implemented; for PM decision)
Make Defend and Repair worth about one attack. Test each change with the same seeded experiment
(target: a defensive policy wins 45–55 % against "aggressive").

1. **Defend braces the castle:** while a character is defending, its team's castle takes half
   block damage, as in S2. This is the most effective single change measured. Consider combining
   it with item 3 or 4, because S2 alone reached 46 %.
2. **Repair 125–150 HP:** necessary but not enough on its own. Also consider a 3-round cooldown,
   or letting Repair rebuild one destroyed block (this changes the "no revival" rule of M2.2, so
   it needs approval).
3. **Make a Defend turn less costly**, for example by letting Defend also grant EXP or shorten
   the defender's special cooldown by 1. This is a new rule, listed only for discussion.
4. **Victory condition:** keep castle loss as an option (`castle_loss_enabled`), but raise it to
   about 70 %, or make elimination primary, if the PM wants characters to matter more. Note S3:
   elimination-only makes matches about twice as long in 3v3, but on its own it does **not** make
   Defend worthwhile.
5. **Whatever changes, keep the AI's defensive preference as the course requires.** Once Defend
   is worth something, that preference becomes a real strength instead of a small handicap.

## 5. How to reproduce
These experiments used throwaway scripts in the session scratchpad: `balance.py`,
`balance_report.py` and `repairable.py`. They are not part of the project. Each builds Battles
with fixed rosters, plays seeded `AIController` (Hard) matches, and patches the S1–S3 variants
in memory. If the PM approves M3.2 balance work, an equivalent script can be added to the
repository so the numbers can be re-run after each change.
